# Related work and positioning

This is a **non-exhaustive** set of primary research references. The proposed system overlaps existing work; no priority claim is made.

1. **Park et al., Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior (2023).** Describes memory, reflection, and planning for believable interactive agents. https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442
2. **Vezhnevets et al., Generative agent-based modeling with Concordia (2023).** Separates acting entities from a simulated environment/game master that resolves consequences. https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03664 and https://github.com/google-deepmind/concordia
3. **Zhou et al., SOTOPIA: Interactive Evaluation for Social Intelligence in Language Agents (2023).** Scenarios and evaluation for complex interactions. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11667
4. **Wang et al., SOTOPIA-π: Interactive Learning of Socially Intelligent Language Agents (2024).** Learns from rated interactive social trajectories and discusses evaluation pitfalls. https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08715
5. **Park et al., Generative Agent Simulations of 1,000 People (2024).** Uses rich interviews to simulate individual attitudes and behaviors. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109
6. **Chen et al., Persona Vectors: Monitoring and Controlling Character Traits in Language Models (2025).** Studies activation-space traits and their changes under training and deployment. https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21509
7. **Peng et al., HEART-Bench: Do LLM Agents Exhibit Human-like Psychology? (2026).** Uses autobiographical episodic memories and decision scenarios for psychological coherence tests. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30058
8. **Wang et al., Do AI Personas Grow? (2026).** Benchmarks how life events affect simulated personality profiles and highlights limits of human-like adaptation. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06485
9. **Xiao et al., The Chameleon's Limit: Investigating Persona Collapse and Homogenization in Large Language Models (2026).** Studies collapse of distinct prompted characters into similar patterns. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24698

## Proposed differentiation

Rather than primarily reproducing human survey responses or simulated social behavior, Decision DNA proposes a **business-facing intervention**: collect evaluated decision histories, distill evidence-linked policies, and measure held-out professional task outcomes against strong static-persona controls. Proposed emphasis includes policy compliance, explicit authority, cross-model portability, economic cost, and real feedback after deployment.

This differentiation will only be a research contribution if empirical results demonstrate it.
